---

## 1. Purpose and naming decision

This pack gives Paeds Resus one coherent way to describe its individual courses, cadre-specific programmes, and institutional products. It is designed for the website, WhatsApp, email drafts, proposals, social posts, brochures, sales conversations, and institutional discovery meetings.

The public name for the institutional learning and continuing professional-development offer should now be:

> **ICPD — Institutional Continuous Professional Development**

Use **ICPD** in public-facing marketing, proposals, sales conversations, landing pages, and institutional navigation. Use **CPD Portal** only as an internal implementation label or when explaining the software layer to a technical buyer. ICPD is the service and operating offer; the portal is the supporting software.

ICPD must not be presented as proof of emergency readiness, bedside competence, IERS duty acceptance, or patient-care performance. IERS remains the institutional emergency-readiness operating system. Training remains the competency-building layer. ICPD records and reports professional-development activity.

**AHA ACLS** develops the structured response skills needed for advanced cardiovascular emergencies, team leadership, rhythm and algorithm-based decision-making, and practical escalation. It is a separate AHA track from the Paeds Resus Fellowship and does not replace paediatric-specific pathways where those are required.

### Website card

**Headline:** Lead the response when the emergency becomes complex.  
**Body:** Strengthen advanced cardiovascular emergency response, team leadership, rhythm-based decision-making, and practical escalation through a structured blended pathway.  
**Price:** **KES 20,000 per person**  
**Cohort rate:** **KES 17,500 per person for cohorts of 7 or more**  
**CTA:** **Book ACLS training**

### WhatsApp message

> **AHA ACLS training — KES 20,000 per person.** Strengthen advanced cardiovascular emergency response, team leadership, and algorithm-based decision-making. For cohorts of **7 or more, the rate is KES 17,500 per person**. Contact Paeds Resus to organise a practical training cohort.

### Institutional sales message

> A hospital’s emergency response is stronger when clinicians share the same language, roles, and escalation habits. Paeds Resus ACLS helps build that common advanced-response foundation at **KES 20,000 per person**, with a cohort rate of **KES 17,500 per person for 7+ learners**.

### Guardrail

Do not market ACLS as a substitute for PALS, NRP, BLS prerequisites, local protocols, or facility emergency systems. State the exact AHA pathway, practical requirements, official credential scope, venue, and inclusions in the final quote.

## 6. NERP — Nurses Emergency Readiness Program

### Core positioning

**NERP** is a nurse-focused emergency-readiness pathway that turns learning into structured participation. It supports nurses through the knowledge, role-based simulation, practical progression, evidence, and assessment steps needed to respond more confidently and consistently to emergencies within their scope and facility context.

### Website card

**Headline:** Equip nurses to recognise, respond, and lead the next safe action.  
**Body:** NERP gives nurses a structured pathway from emergency knowledge to role-based simulation and practical readiness. Build capability that fits the realities of busy wards, emergency units, and low-resource facilities.  
**Price:** **Request the current NERP offer**  
**CTA:** **Explore NERP**

### WhatsApp message

> **NERP — Nurses Emergency Readiness Program.** A structured Paeds Resus pathway for nurses who need more than a certificate: practical emergency learning, role-based simulations, progression evidence, and clear next actions. Ask us for the current NERP cohort offer.

### Institutional sales message

> Your nurses are often the first clinicians to recognise deterioration. NERP gives them a shared readiness pathway for learning, simulation, role clarity, and practical progression — designed to support safe action within scope, not to replace facility policy or clinical judgement.

### Guardrail

Do not claim that NERP independently certifies a nurse for every emergency procedure or guarantees patient outcomes. Keep the language focused on structured learning, readiness, practical progression, and scope-appropriate performance.

## 7. IERP — Interns Emergency Readiness Program

### Core positioning

**IERP** is a profile-first emergency-readiness pathway for medical interns. It combines structured cognitive learning, confirmed role-based online simulations, practical progression, and evidence gates so interns develop a disciplined approach to emergency teamwork before they face increasingly complex clinical responsibility.

### Website card

**Headline:** Give every intern a safer start in emergency care.  
**Body:** IERP guides medical interns through structured emergency learning, confirmed team roles, online simulations, practical progression, and evidence-based readiness.  
**Price:** **Request the current IERP offer**  
**CTA:** **Explore IERP**

### WhatsApp message

> **IERP — Interns Emergency Readiness Program.** Help interns build emergency habits early: structured learning, named team roles, confirmed simulations, practical progression, and evidence that can be reviewed. Ask us about the next IERP intake.

### Institutional sales message

> Intern orientation should include more than a timetable and a badge. IERP gives interns a governed pathway for emergency learning, simulation participation, practical progression, and evidence review — while keeping institutional access and clinical scope clearly defined.

### Guardrail

Do not confuse IERP with IERS. **IERP is the intern training pathway; IERS is the institutional emergency-readiness system.** Do not describe IERP as independent clinical authorisation or as a replacement for supervision, facility protocols, or professional regulation.

**Price:** **Quoted by cohort and scope**

### WhatsApp message

> **ILSP — Institutional Life Support Program.** A managed facility cohort for life-support learning, delivery, practical assessment, and completion evidence. We scope the programme around your staff list, departments, schedule, and readiness priorities. Contact us for an institutional quotation.

### Institutional sales message

## 9. IERS — Institutional Emergency Readiness System

### Core positioning

**IERS** is Paeds Resus’s hospital-wide emergency-readiness operating layer. It connects emergency-response governance, team activation, roster and role clarity, equipment readiness, drills, evidence review, corrective actions, ResusGPS support, and aggregate reporting. It is readiness infrastructure plus training coordination — not a certificate-seat catalogue.

### Website card

**Headline:** Make emergency readiness visible, owned, and improvable.  
**Body:** IERS helps hospitals organise emergency-response roles, readiness checks, activation evidence, equipment gaps, drills, corrective actions, and leadership review in one governed operating system.  
**Price:** **KES 200,000 per year**  
**CTA:** **Book an emergency-readiness conversation**

### Executive message

> **IERS — KES 200,000 per year.** Move from emergency policies that sit on a shelf to a working readiness system. IERS connects team activation, role clarity, equipment readiness, drills, evidence review, corrective actions, and leadership reporting, with paediatric priority framing.

### Institutional sales message

> Most facilities do not need another isolated certificate campaign. They need to know which teams are covered, which roles are accepted, whether critical equipment is ready, how quickly the response begins, which gaps remain open, and who owns the fix. IERS provides the operating layer for that work at **KES 200,000 per year**.

### Guardrail

Do not claim that IERS alone reduces mortality or guarantees successful resuscitation. Use measurable process language: activation count, time to first responder, equipment fixes closed, training coverage, drill performance, and Care Signal improvement actions. IERS does not replace clinical records, emergency services, approved facility protocols, or professional judgement.

## 10. ICPD — Institutional Continuous Professional Development

### Naming and definition

**ICPD** is the managed institutional service for continuous professional development. It helps a facility organise, record, verify, track, and report professional-development activity across staff groups and departments.

> **ICPD measures learning activity and professional development. IERS measures emergency readiness. They work together, but they are not the same product.**

### Website card

**Headline:** Turn staff learning into a trusted institutional record.  
**Body:** ICPD gives hospitals one clear way to organise professional-development sessions, verify attendance, track targets, issue certificates from verified records, and produce leadership reports across departments.  
**Price:** **KES 200,000 per year**  
**CTA:** **Start your ICPD programme**

### Short institutional message

> **ICPD — Institutional Continuous Professional Development, KES 200,000 per year.** Bring staff learning into one accountable system: scheduled sessions, verified attendance, development targets, certificates, and leadership reporting.

### Education and HR message

> Your teams are already learning. ICPD helps the institution prove what happened, who attended, which targets are progressing, and where development gaps remain. Organise professional development across departments without confusing attendance records with emergency-readiness evidence.

### Leadership message

> Give leadership a reliable view of institutional learning activity. ICPD connects sessions, verified attendance, targets, certificates, and reports while preserving department-level accountability and audit history.

### What ICPD includes

| ICPD capability | Customer-facing wording |
|---|---|
| Session lifecycle | Draft, schedule, open, review attendance, close, issue certificates, and archive with an audit trail |
| Verified attendance | Count participation only after attendance is reviewed and verified |
| Department and cadre scope | Direct sessions to the right staff group without relying on ambiguous labels |
| Targets | Set and monitor professional-development targets by institution, department, cadre, or defined audience |
| Certificates | Issue certificates from closed sessions with verified attendance |
| Reporting | Produce leadership reports with period, denominator, source, target, gap, and data-quality context |
| Governance | Preserve corrections, voids, exports, and lifecycle changes in an auditable record |

### Guardrail

Never say that ICPD proves a provider is ready for an emergency, competent at the bedside, assigned to an IERS duty, or responsible for a patient-care outcome. Say that ICPD records and reports professional-development activity.

## 11. Supporting product messages

### Paeds Resus Fellowship

> **Build paediatric emergency depth one condition at a time.** The Paeds Resus Fellowship is a unified online pathway of focused micro-courses covering the conditions and patterns that broad certification courses cannot exhaust. Start with the emergencies you see most, build breadth across domains, and progress toward Paeds Resus Fellow identity.

### ResusGPS

> **The next safe action, when time matters.** ResusGPS provides structured paediatric emergency reference support for assessment, prioritisation, weight-based calculations, CPR timing, and reassessment prompts. It supports trained providers and does not replace local protocols, clinical judgement, supervision, or emergency services.

### Care Signal

> **Turn near-misses into safer systems.** Care Signal supports honest, blameless reporting and institutional review of near-misses, incidents, and improvement actions. It is a quality-improvement layer, not a punitive reporting tool and not a substitute for a formal clinical record.

### Cohort and institutional readiness conversation

> **Start with the gap, not the seat count.** Paeds Resus helps a facility map staff roles, emergency-response priorities, training needs, professional-development activity, readiness evidence, and the first 90 days of implementation before finalising scope.

## 12. Cross-sell journeys

### Individual provider journey

1. Start with **AHA BLS** as the universal foundation.
2. Add **AHA ACLS** where the provider’s role and pathway require advanced cardiovascular response.
3. Use the **Paeds Resus Fellowship** to build condition-specific paediatric depth.
4. Use **ResusGPS** as structured bedside reference support within the appropriate plan.

### Nurse pathway

1. Begin with **AHA BLS**.
2. Progress into **NERP** for structured nurse-specific readiness and role-based practice.
3. Add relevant **Fellowship** micro-courses for common paediatric emergencies.
4. Connect the individual pathway to an institution using **IERS** and **ICPD** where the facility adopts them.

### Intern pathway

1. Complete the individual profile and eligibility requirements.
2. Enter **IERP** for structured learning, confirmed simulations, and practical progression.
3. Use targeted **Fellowship** micro-courses to strengthen recurring clinical gaps.
4. Join the institution’s wider readiness and development environment through **IERS** and **ICPD**, without confusing the products.

### Hospital pathway

1. Begin with an **IERS readiness conversation** and baseline.
2. Use **ILSP**, BLS, ACLS, and other agreed training pathways to close role-mapped competency gaps.
3. Use **ICPD** to organise and report wider staff professional development.
4. Use **ResusGPS** and Care Signal to support bedside reference and improvement loops.
5. Review process measures and open actions at 30, 60, and 90 days.

## 14. Claims and compliance guardrails

Use **“helps,” “supports,” “builds,” “organises,” “tracks,” “makes visible,”** and **“provides a structured pathway.”** Avoid guaranteed outcome language such as “prevents deaths,” “eliminates emergencies,” “guarantees competence,” or “makes your hospital safe.” The mission may be described as reducing preventable harm, but public claims should remain tied to measurable process evidence until a governed outcomes evaluation supports stronger language.

Keep these distinctions exact:

| Do say | Do not say |
|---|---|
| Paeds Resus is the organisation and platform | ResusGPS is the name of every Paeds Resus product |
| Paeds Resus Limited delivers the contracted training entity work | Every Paeds Resus course is automatically an official AHA credential |
| IERP is the Interns Emergency Readiness Program | IERP is the Institutional Emergency Readiness System |
| IERS is the Institutional Emergency Readiness System | IERS is a CPD tracker or certificate bundle |
| ICPD records professional-development activity | ICPD proves emergency readiness or bedside competence |
| ResusGPS is bedside clinical decision support | ResusGPS replaces protocols, emergency services, or judgement |
| IERS is readiness infrastructure plus training coordination | IERS is merely bulk AHA certification |

## 15. Recommended rollout order

**Now:** Publish the BLS and ACLS individual messages with the approved prices and cohort rates. Replace public “Institutional CPD” wording with **ICPD — Institutional Continuous Professional Development** on institutional pages, proposals, and sales materials.

**Next:** Create separate landing-page sections for NERP, IERP, ILSP, IERS, and ICPD. Each page should show the audience, problem, inclusions, exclusions, pricing status, implementation path, and call to action.

**Then:** Build one institutional comparison page showing the three components separately: competency training, IERS activation, and ICPD. Add a readiness-conversation form rather than forcing hospitals into a generic seat checkout.

**Delay:** Publishing a final NERP, IERP, or ILSP price until leadership confirms the commercial offer and scope. Do not create discounts by copying the BLS/ACLS cohort rates into those programmes.

**Ignore:** Any old copy that treats the former CPD Portal label as the public product name, collapses IERS into training seats, or describes AHA, Fellowship, IERP, NERP, and ResusGPS as one undifferentiated programme.
